# Landmark Church — migrating off Subsplash to a connected Webflow CMS and AI-powered search

## The situation
Landmark Church (Ocean City / Egg Harbor Township, NJ) ran an active content operation —
sermons, notes, podcasts, events, groups — on Subsplash for the website, stacked on top of
Planning Center for actual church management. Subsplash is template-based with no real CMS:
every content change routed through one team, and sermon notes lived in a separate, unsearchable
account only ever shared once via a direct link.

## What we built
Migrated the site fully off Subsplash onto Webflow, keeping Planning Center as the system of
record underneath. One connected CMS for team, groups, events, service times, and campus info
with real permissions. Pulled Planning Center events/groups/classes and the YouTube sermon
library directly into the site. Added a member dashboard for saving sermons/notes/resources,
built mobile-first, automated publishing through the Webflow CMS API, added FAQs sitewide for
SEO/AI-answer visibility, and built a custom AI search tool ("Landmark Answer") directly into
the site. Custom designed in Figma with GSAP/Lottie motion throughout. Tier: Custom +
Integrations. Launched April 22, 2026 (later than the original proposal window — the delay was
client-side, not execution, so this isn't the project to cite for speed).

## Results
All verified via GA4/Webflow analytics, 90 days post-launch vs. 90 days pre-launch, non-human
traffic excluded:
- Active users +31.3% (6,924 → 9,092); sessions +39.6% (12,777 → 17,843)
- Mobile sessions +67.0%, growing from 60.7% to 73.5% of all traffic
- Organic search sessions +29.5% (5,604 → 7,256) with no paid spend
- Sermon Notes sessions 59 → 2,799 (+4,644%); total sermon content traffic +27.5%
- Local market growth: Ocean City users +135% (188 → 442)
- Cut recurring platform costs ~65-75%: ~$3,000-4,000/year (Subsplash) down to $924/year
  (Webflow Business + Localize + Analyze add-ons, plus tax) — saving an estimated
  $2,100-3,100/year
- Individual sermon notes get opened 100-240+ times each, well after the week they were
  delivered — the old single-share-link model couldn't do this at all
